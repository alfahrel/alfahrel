"Notes from the Underground" is Dostoevsky's most revolutionary novel. One of the most remarkable characters in literature, the unnamed narrator is a former clerk who resolutely withdraws to underground life. In complete seclusion from society, he wrote a passionate, obsessive, contradictory narrative that served as a devastating attack on social utopianism and an affirmation of essentially irrational human nature.
