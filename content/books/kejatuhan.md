In a bar in Amsterdam, a man named Jean-Baptiste Clamence makes a confession to a stranger. He reminisces about his past life as a respected judge, has high self-esteem, adheres to libertine ideology, but is immune to the judgments—portrait of a modern human being. In the end Clamence realizes that no one is innocent, and therefore no one can judge others from the standpoint of truth.
