In 1845, Thoreau moved to the woods near his hometown of Concord, Massachusetts. He lived in a cabin he built himself, on the edge of Walden Pond. Walden is Thoreau's autobiographical work about an experiment in simple and independent living in the wilderness for two years. Thoreau believed that a life focused on essential things and in harmony with nature is more fulfilling than the pursuit of wealth.
